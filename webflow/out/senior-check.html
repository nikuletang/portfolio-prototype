An HCI course project at the University of Maryland. Our team of five interviewed five senior residents of Prince George's County, modeled what we heard, and proposed wireframes for signing up for the Senior ID card and activities online. The designs are proposals from a class project, not a launched product.

01Project overview

A free program that many eligible residents weren't using

The program and its audience

The Prince George's County Department of Parks and Recreation runs a Senior Services program for residents aged 60 and older. It offers free activities, events, nutrition services, and access to pools and fitness rooms across the county's 40 community centers. Seniors can sign up for a physical Senior ID card, which the department uses to track visits to its facilities. The card isn't required to enter a building or join an event.

The challenge

The department wanted more eligible residents to hold a Senior ID card, while offering services that fit the wide range of seniors' needs and lifestyles.

At the time of the project, the brief stated that between 100,000 and 125,000 county residents were eligible, and that 15,000 to 17,000 held a Senior ID card.

These figures are as stated in the project materials in fall 2020. They aren't current, and the original source isn't cited here.

Five conversations with senior residents

We interviewed five senior residents about how they find, choose and sign up for activities, and how the department's Senior Services fit into their lives.

About the sample

Five interviews is a small, qualitative sample. The findings describe what these participants told us. They don't speak for all older adults or for every Senior Services member.

What participants told us, and what we took from it

Each finding is what we heard in the interviews. The need beside it is the team's interpretation.

01

Finding information online was hard

What participants described

Participants struggled to navigate the department's website and find what they were looking for.

Need we identified

A user-friendly website with a streamlined process for the most important tasks.

02

The pandemic left many feeling isolated

What participants described

Many participants described feeling socially isolated during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Need we identified

Ways to connect with others through events and activities.

03

Staying active mattered

What participants described

Participants said that staying active was important to them.

Need we identified

Activities that keep them physically active.

04

Strong ties to the community

What participants described

Participants were community-oriented and had strong ties to Prince George's County.

Need we identified

Opportunities to serve their community, and activities to do with family and friends.

04Synthesis

Turning interviews into models

We used several UX models to find patterns in the interview data: shared themes, the people who shape seniors' engagement, the steps of signing up, and who seniors are. These insights then shaped the designs.

Team artifact

Affinity diagram

We grouped notes from the five interviews into an affinity diagram to find common themes, attitudes and needs across participants. The four findings in section 03 came from this board.

My model

Relationship model

The interviews showed that the people in seniors' lives influence whether they engage with Senior Services. I created a relationship model that maps three levels of relationships that influence participants' engagement with the department and the kinds of activities they do, and shows how those relationships could be used to meet their needs.

Senior program activity buddies

Spouse, Club 300 buddies, Senior Green Team, grandkids

Senior program acquaintances

Parks & Rec staff, golfers

External activity influencers

Neighbors, local friends

From the inner level to the outer one, people share less interaction with the Parks and Recreation department.

Team artifact

Journey map

We mapped how a senior might sign up for an activity online, with their thoughts and emotions, pain points and opportunities at each step: consulting friends and family, searching online, calling the department, and assessing the ID program.

Active Agatha is a persona, a composite we built from the interview data. She isn't one of the participants, and the quotes on the map are the persona's synthesized thoughts.

The map's "Opportunities" and "Metrics" boxes were proposals. The metrics were suggested ways to measure a future program. They weren't collected.

Team artifact

Identity model

The identity model groups traits we heard into three parts, I like, I do and I am, with what each trait asks of a service:

I like

The outdoors, social interaction, family time

I do

Morning activities, web searches

I am

In the dark, an eager learner, proudly analog

05Opportunity framing

From what we heard to what we could build

We did a wall walk of the affinity diagram, asking what we must address, support or solve to add value to participants' lives. Most issues were about the website and about social isolation during the pandemic. We then listed “hot ideas”: possible design solutions.

1 · What participants described

Issues

  1. 01Couldn't find the activity list or schedule on the website.
  2. 02Didn't know how to sign up for the Senior ID online.
  3. 03Didn't receive mail or email from the department.
  4. 04Felt socially isolated during the pandemic.

Compiled by the team from the interviews.

2 · Team opportunity

What to address

Make programs and schedules easy to find and join online, guide people through Senior ID sign-up, let people choose how they hear from the department, and help seniors connect during the pandemic.

This is a summary of the wall walk's focus, not a separate team artifact.

06Ideation and visioning

Sketching the most promising ideas

With the issues and hot ideas in hand, we chose three hot ideas for a visioning exercise and sketched how each could work. We then decided which would add the most value to seniors' lives and designed wireframes for those.

Which ideas reached the wireframes

Wireframed
  • The Senior ID vision: a landing page, a five-step registration with activity sign-up, and returning-user pages.
Linked, not designed
  • Mental health support and Senior Ambassadors appear as quick links on the landing page. Their pages weren't wireframed.
  • A Learn More video slot stands in for the video guide.
Not carried forward
  • The prospect form doesn't appear in the sketches or wireframes shown here.
